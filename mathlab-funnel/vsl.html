It starts with “um…”

Before a kid says “I don’t like math,” they hesitate.

Hesitation looks like:
  • “8 × 6 is… um… 48.”
  • “1/4 is… um…”
  • “0.5 equals… pause…”
  • Multi-digit subtraction = slow + unsure
  • 997 vs 1,002—closer to 1,000?
What happens next:
  • Basics aren’t automatic → math takes too much effort
  • Effort becomes frustration
  • Frustration becomes avoidance
  • A year later: “I don’t like math.”
Not because they can’t. Because the basics were never fully locked in.

Not all math skills are equal

Schools often treat standards as equal. Real life doesn’t.

Math lives or dies on foundations:
Add/Sub fluency Mult/Div automaticity Fractions ↔ Decimals equivalence Estimation & magnitude Reasonableness checks

If these are solid, math stays smooth. If they’re shaky, the break is inevitable when math gets heavier.
