Fix the lowest rung first.

A 7-day, 5-minutes-a-day reset that helps you identify the one skill slowing your child down — and fix that first, instead of trying to fix everything at once.

Quick context: Math is a ladder. If one lower rung isn’t automatic yet, everything above it takes more effort. School keeps moving (pacing guides), and home often becomes “school part two.” This reset removes pressure by fixing one rung at a time.

The 7-Day Reset (simple steps)

  1. Pick one rung. Start with the first skill that causes slowdown. For many kids, this is two-digit minus one-digit subtraction, but it could also be addition, multiplication, or division facts.
  2. Practice verbally for 5 minutes a day. Ask quick questions out loud. No worksheets. No fingers. No counting strategies.
  3. Use one success rule. You’re looking for answers in ~2 seconds without pausing, whisper-counting, or using fingers.
  4. Don’t stack skills. Avoid practicing “whatever happened in class today.” Stay on the same rung until it’s easy — then move up.
Why this works: Math is hierarchical. When kids try to learn new skills before old ones are automatic, they experience learning interference — progress feels random and slow. Fix one rung first, and everything above it becomes easier.
