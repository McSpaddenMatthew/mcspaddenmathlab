What happens when the low rung isn’t automatic

This is what parents usually see when one foundational skill isn’t automatic yet:

  • Multi-step problems break because the child runs out of working memory.
  • Accuracy drops as the pace increases (tests, timed work, longer assignments).
  • Confidence erodes even when the child “understands” the lesson.
  • Homework becomes a fight because everything takes too long.

Quick context (in plain English)

Math is a ladder. If a lower rung isn’t automatic yet, everything above it costs more effort. School keeps moving. This reset works because you fix one rung at a time until it’s easy.

The 7-Day Reset (simple steps)

  1. Pick one “slowdown” skill. Choose the first place your child hesitates. Common culprits: subtraction with regrouping, addition facts, multiplication facts, division facts.
  2. Do 5 minutes a day (out loud). Fast, verbal questions. No worksheets. No apps required.
  3. Use one success rule. You’re aiming for answers in about 2 seconds without fingers, whisper-counting, or long pauses.
  4. Don’t stack skills. Stay on the same rung until it’s easy. Then move up one rung.
Why this works: When a basic skill is slow, the brain spends all its energy on the “little” part, and there’s not enough left for the “big” part (multi-step thinking). Make the low rung automatic, and the higher work gets easier.
